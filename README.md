# bonitabluehair.com

Can Bonita Bluehair hit level 60 in WoW: Forever before Christmas? This site keeps score.

Three pages, one tab each: the race (`index.html`), the record (`stats.html`) and the hourglass (`hourglass.html`). The hourglass needs no data of its own: its top bulb holds the share of the time from launch to the deadline that's left (by the sand's area, so the level drops faster as the bulb narrows), and a grain drips from the neck every fraction of a second, every 1.2 s or so when the viewer's device asks for reduced motion. It reads `data/history.json` only for the dates, his current level, and whether he has hit 60, which stops the sand where it was. After the deadline the top is empty and the drip stops.

- **Site:** `index.html` reads `data/history.json` and draws everything in the browser.
- **Updates:** `.github/workflows/update.yml` calls `scripts/update.py`, which reads Blizzard's official World of Warcraft API and retries through rate limits. WoW: Forever is the `forever` game version there, and its characters have a first and last name and no realm, so he is `/profile/wow/character/bonita-bluehair` in the namespace `profile-forever-us` (summary, `/equipment`, `/character-media`, `/specializations`, `/pvp-summary`, `/professions`, `/reputations`, `/achievements/statistics`), and his guild's roster is `/data/wow/guild/<guildSlug>/roster`. Only the summary (level and last logout) is essential; any other part that fails is skipped and its last known values are kept. The API client's ID and secret are the repository Actions secrets `BLIZZARD_CLIENT_ID` and `BLIZZARD_CLIENT_SECRET` (created by the owner at https://develop.battle.net/access/clients; nobody else should handle the secret, and the script never prints them or the token). `scripts/blizzard_probe.py` (the "Blizzard API probe" workflow, run by hand) is how the addresses were found: it prints the status and shape of every reply and saves the full replies as a three-day download.
  - **Why Blizzard's API.** Until Nov 14, 2026 the updater read the same data through classicarmory.gg's internal feed. From the Nov 14 20:07 UTC check that feed answered `403 Forbidden` to outside programs while the site's own pages kept working. That is its owner's call, so the tracker did not work around it (no browser disguise, cookies or copied tokens): checks were paused (`PAUSED` in `update.py`, and a `"paused"` entry in `data/history.json` that makes both pages say so) until the first check through Blizzard's API on Nov 15, which removes the entry. The data is the same underneath: on Nov 15 Blizzard's replies matched the saved gear, professions, reputations, item level, logout time and all 61 record stats.
  - **Level between logouts.** His character sheet (level, gear, stats) only refreshes when he logs out, so the updater also reads his level from the guild roster, which follows the game while he is online. When the roster is ahead of every saved snapshot, it adds a level snapshot stamped with the time of that check and marked `"source": "roster"` (the first was level 34 on Nov 11, 2026, when the sheet had said 33 for hours); his next logout adds its own snapshot as usual, and gear, stats and the record still wait for that logout. The summary also gives his XP into the current level, saved as `character.xp`; nothing on the pages uses it yet.
  - **Gear reel.** It saves a new frame whenever his equipped gear or his picture changes, commits, and redeploys GitHub Pages (only when something was saved). Blizzard redraws his picture later than it updates his gear, sometimes days later (on Nov 12 its newest was from Nov 10 02:19 UTC, two gear changes behind), so a gear change is saved right away with the last picture (`"stalePic": true`, with a note on the page) and the new picture is swapped into that frame once it arrives. A frame is only marked that way when something the picture shows changed, so not for a necklace, ring, trinket or relic. Blizzard also re-renders him now and then with a few dozen pixels different (mostly eye glow), which changes the file's hash but not the look: when the gear list is unchanged and under 0.2% of pixels differ from `renders/latest.png`, no frame is added, and a visibly different picture for the same logout replaces that frame's picture instead of adding a twin (on Nov 10, 2026 two near-identical redraws had left two frames for one logout; repaired Nov 12). Blizzard's picture server also answers from more than one copy of his file, and a copy can be days behind: on Nov 18, 2026 about half the hourly checks got his Nov 16 picture back in place of the one drawn that morning, and the reel filed it as a new look three times (repaired Nov 18). So a picture that differs from the reel's newest is ignored when it is an older one: its file date (the `Last-Modified` header, saved on each frame as `picAt`) is earlier than the newest picture's, or, without dates, an earlier frame already has it. The log line says so. A frame can also carry a hand-written `note` about its picture, shown under it: the Nov 16 frame has one because Blizzard's own picture of that gear has a black face.
  - **Record, professions and reputation.** Saved to `data/stats.json` in the layout and order they have always had (the updater reorders Blizzard's replies to match, so a check that changes nothing changes nothing in the files). When a reply leaves something out, the last known values are kept instead of being wiped. Two leftovers from the classicarmory.gg period: the Nov 8 16:30 and Nov 9 07:09 UTC stats snapshots carry the Nov 8 04:29 profession and reputation values (that feed had moved them and the updater followed late), and six record stats that feed stopped sending were held at their last values from Nov 9 to Nov 15 ("Professions at maximum skill", "Secondary skills at maximum skill", "Weapon skills at maximum skill", "First Aid skill", "Most Alliance factions at Exalted" and "Bazzalan kills (Ragefire Chasm)"). Blizzard's API sends all six, so they are live again; its "First Aid skill" statistic reads 330 against a real skill of 238, which is Blizzard's number, not a tracker error.

GitHub's schedule stays on as a backup and does nothing when the last check was under 40 minutes ago. It has been on since Nov 9, 2026 (first run 23:07 UTC).   2. From `votes-worker/`: `npx wrangler@4.152.0 secret put GITHUB_DISPATCH_TOKEN` and paste the token, then `npx wrangler@4.152.0 deploy`.

- **The 3D stage:** on, with `models/bonita-2026-11-26.glb` (0.5 MB, exported at level 49 with the helm, shoulders, chest, bracers, legs and staff from his Nov 25 14:41 Eastern logout; earlier models: Nov 7 at level 23, Nov 9 at level 32, Nov 12 at level 35, Nov 14 at level 38, Nov 16 at level 40, Nov 18 at level 44, Nov 22 at level 46), exported with wow.export 0.2.21 from a local WoW: Forever install: Night Elf female, appearance matched to the Armory render by eye (the Armory doesn't record it), gear from his item IDs (run the "Blizzard API probe" workflow and read `equipment.json` in its download; the saved gear frames hold names only), idle clip only. wow.export leaves the helm, staff and shoulders unattached at the origin; `tools/model/fix-attach.mjs` puts the helm on its head bone, the staff on its hand bone and the shoulder pads on the collarbones (it warns about any loose piece it has no rule for, and drops an item's effect pieces, such as a helm lamp's glow, which wow.export exports wearing the body texture) (on the upper-arm attachment bones they swing down with the arm), and turns the model to face the camera. wow.export also doesn't load cape textures for this client, so the cape comes out wearing the body's texture; the script takes the cape's own texture as a PNG and gives the cape its own material (`export-kit/step-cape-texture.js` looks it up from the item id in `<BBH_IO>/cape-item.txt` and saves it as `<BBH_IO>/cape-<file id>.png`: ItemModifiedAppearance → ItemAppearance → ItemDisplayInfo → its material resource → the `item/objectcomponents/cape/` file). The idle always plays, like the race chart, and pauses only off screen. This is extracted game art, which Blizzard has warned can draw takedowns; to turn it off, set `"model": null` in the manifest. The page only marks the model stale when a newer gear-reel frame lists different drawn gear than the manifest's `gear`. Necklaces, rings, trinkets and relics aren't drawn on a character and have no slot in the export, so the page leaves those slots (Neck, Finger, Trinket, Relic) out of the comparison and the manifest's `gear` lists drawn items only. When `models/manifest.json` has a `"model"`, the race page loads `vendor/model-viewer-4.4.0.min.js` (Google, Apache 2.0; meshopt decoder from meshoptimizer 1.2.0, MIT; both checked byte for byte against npm) after the page has loaded, fades the model in over the render, and lets people turn him by drag or arrow keys. It falls back to the render on any failure, offers "Load 3D" on Save-Data or slow connections (or always, with `"autoload": false`), and, when the gear reel has a newer frame than `exportedAt` with different drawn gear, keeps his current picture up with one quiet line under it: "3D update pending" and a "View {date} model" link that loads the older model. (Until Nov 16, 2026 that state was a panel reading "The 3D model still has his {date} gear" with a "Show 3D anyway" button, which read as a fault; he changes gear every day or two, so it is a normal state.) To turn it on:
  1. Export his model as glTF with animations (wow.export 0.2.21 on a PC with WoW: Forever installed; it has worked seven times with build 1.60.1.70862; `step-import.js` waits for the Characters tab to finish loading before it imports, since importing earlier left the tab stuck on "Loading character shaders" on Nov 22, 2026). The scripts in `tools/model/export-kit/` drive it without clicking: unzip the portable build (`portable-wow-export-win-x64-0.2.21.zip` from the wow.export releases, sha256 `a8a8ed40bbbba437672f16b75ed2505a74b4174f80e59316a67864196b20416d`), copy `bbh-auto.js` into its `src/` folder and add `<script src="bbh-auto.js"></script>` before `</body>` in `src/index.html`, start `wow.export.exe --disable-auto-update` with the environment variable `BBH_IO` set to an empty folder, write the dressing-room link from `node wh-encode.mjs '<slot: item id map>' <level>` into `<BBH_IO>/import-url.txt`, then send `step-import.js`, `step-look.js` and `step-export.js` in turn with `wx.ps1 -Dir <BBH_IO> -File <step>` (`step-attachments.js` lists the model's attachment points when `fix-attach.mjs` needs a new rule). Keep the unzipped app, the cape PNG and `node_modules` somewhere that isn't the system Temp folder: on Nov 15, 2026 Windows' cleanup deleted everything there older than seven days, including the app and these scripts, which is why the scripts now live in the repo. In the Characters tab: import his gear, set his appearance (Skin Color 806, Face 825, Hair Style 844 "Loose Tail", Hair Color 863, Markings 873 "Crane", Markings Color 8177), and in Custom Geoset Control switch off Headdress (3701), FacialJewelry (1601) and Necklace (3601), which come switched on and aren't in his Armory render. Miss that last part and the model wears a circlet and necklace (three extra mesh nodes and a `nightelffemale_hd_3621913` material in the result). Using a model taken from the game files is the owner's call: Blizzard has warned that datamined assets can draw takedowns.
  2. `cd tools/model && npm install`, then `npx gltf-transform copy bonita.gltf raw.glb`, `node keep-idle.mjs raw.glb idle.glb 1` (clip 1 is Stand), `node fix-attach.mjs idle.glb fixed.glb cape.png` (leave the PNG off if he has no cape; the script warns about any loose piece it doesn't know how to attach; `node check-attach.mjs fixed.glb` prints where each attached piece sits against the body, for when the page can't be looked at), and `npx gltf-transform optimize fixed.glb bonita-YYYY-MM-DD.glb --compress meshopt --texture-compress webp --texture-size 1024 --simplify false --flatten false --join false --instance false --palette false` (aim for under 1 MB).
  3. Put the `.glb` in `models/` (and delete the old one), then fill in `manifest.json`: `model`, `bytes`, `exportedAt`, `gearAsOf`, `gear` (the item names from the newest look in `data/history.json`, leaving out the Neck, Finger, Trinket and Relic slots), and the framing (`orientation`, `cameraOrbit`, `cameraTarget`, `fieldOfView`) so his feet land on the ground disc and his head in the halo ring. On localhost, `?model=`, `?look=YYYY-MM-DD` and `?sim=savedata|nowebgl` preview the other states.
  4. Export again when the page says the model still has his older gear, which means a new gear-reel frame changed a drawn item. A frame that only changes a necklace, ring, trinket or relic needs no export. If the drawn items are the same but the names in the manifest have drifted, update `gear` and `gearAsOf` instead.
- **Day and night sky:** both page headers follow the viewer's local time of day: the sun's height is worked out from the date and clock (40°N, solar noon at 12:00), the sky blends through night, blue hour, dawn/dusk, golden hour and day, and the moon sets as the sun rises in the same corner, clear of the text. The two discs share one spot, so they hand off rather than cross: the moon has faded out by the time the sun is 4° below the horizon and the sun starts to show at 3° below, which leaves the corner empty for about five minutes at dawn and dusk (until Nov 12, 2026 their fades overlapped and both showed at once around sunrise and sunset). Hero labels lighten in daylight to keep their contrast. Preview another time on localhost with `?sky=HH:MM`.
- **Change the deadline:** edit `deadline` in `data/history.json` (ISO time, UTC). It's set to midnight Eastern on Dec 25.

Pace math uses the Classic XP table, with play time per level estimated as XP ÷ (45 + 5 × level).
